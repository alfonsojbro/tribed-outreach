The human touch is the most expensive thing you can sell. Most apps can't sell it at all.

Almost every coach I build an app for asks the same first question: what should the subscription cost? I tell them the subscription is the smallest part of the answer.

Perry Marshall has a name for this in 80/20 Sales and Marketing: the espresso machine principle. For every 1,000 people buying a $5 latte, one of them will happily pay $2,700 for the espresso machine. If you don't sell the machine, that money walks out the door and gets spent somewhere else.

Most consumer apps never sell the machine. One low tier, $9 a month, and the ceiling on lifetime value is set on day one. RevenueCat's latest State of Subscription Apps report found high-priced apps pull around three times more lifetime value per paying customer than low-priced ones.

An app with a real expert behind it doesn't have that ceiling. The subscription covers the program, the community, the AI coach trained on your method. And when someone wants the whole machinery, the 1:1 work, the cohort, the done-with-you tier, there is a human who can deliver it. The app watches for those people and puts the offer in front of them automatically.

You keep the $9 members and stop losing the $2,700 ones.
