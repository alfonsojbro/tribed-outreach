# Approved answers — the recurring post-demo questions

Read this from Mode 2 (references/replies.md) and Mode 5 (references/postdemo.md) whenever a prospect's reply asks one of the five questions below. Added 2026-09-13.

**Why this file exists.** Warm prospects were dying on reply latency, not on copy. Tony Omary waited 51 days for pricing tiers and ownership terms. Marnus Marx waited 39 days after nine specific questions. Angela Soltan asked four of these same questions on 2026-09-12. The LinkedIn funnel read `interested` 112 → `meeting` 5 on 2026-09-13. Every one of those threads stalled because the run had no approved answer and escalated the whole reply to Alfonso. This file holds the approved answers, so the run drafts the reply itself and Alfonso only fills a blank when one is genuinely his to fill.

## How to use it

1. **Match.** The reply asks one of the five questions below, in any wording or language. A reply that asks several of them (Angela's four, Marnus's nine) gets ONE message answering each in the order asked.
2. **Draft fresh.** The facts come from here; the wording is written new every time in the prospect's register and language (SKILL.md language rules, references/voice.md). The samples show register. Never paste them.
3. **Label the draft.** Every reply draft built from this file carries one of two labels, in chat when the draft is shown and in the run note:
   - **`ready`** — every fact in the message is confirmed below and no `[ALFONSO TO CONFIRM]` blank is touched. Queue it like any reply (`queue_outreach_drafts`, `slot: "reply"`, pending for the dashboard). It does NOT go in the report's "Waiting on Alfonso" line. Nothing is auto-sent; a human still approves it in the dashboard, but nobody has to write it.
   - **`needs Alfonso`** — the message touches a `[ALFONSO TO CONFIRM]` blank. Write the WHOLE reply anyway, leave the blank exactly as marked, queue it, and list it under "Waiting on Alfonso" naming the blank ("<prospect>: <the blank's text>"). He fills the blank and approves. He does not write the reply.
4. **Never fill a blank.** A guess about export format, listing ownership or configuration ownership is a promise made in Alfonso's name to a real prospect. The blank stays a blank until he replaces it in this file.
5. **Never go past the facts.** Anything a prospect asks that is not on the fact list below falls under the existing rule (references/postdemo.md, "Facts rule"): do not invent, ask Alfonso. Questions seen more than once that are NOT yet here, from `wiki/Coach Buying Questions.md`: where clients book, whether the app replaces their website, content import (articles, reviews), offline use, and whether their material is ever used outside their own app (the exposure fear in the Marnus note). Candidates for the next additions to this file.
6. **A written-scope ask is still an artifact.** When the prospect asks for a proposal or a written scope (Marnus: "make it concrete..."), the rule in references/replies.md holds: build the proposal page and send the link. The approved answers supply the DM cover line and the proposal's content; they do not replace the page.

Pre-demo replies: SKILL.md's "hold the number back" rule still applies to a cold price question before the prospect has looked at anything. The price answer below is for AFTER they have seen the demo, or for any price question that has already sat unanswered for 48 hours, because at that point the deal is stalling without it, which is exactly the last-resort moment SKILL.md describes.

## The facts (the only product facts approved for these replies)

- **Price:** $149 per month. Setup is free. Month to month, cancel whenever. **There is one price. No tiers exist in any approved answer.** Confirmed by the sent price (Decisions Log 2026-08-14: "Tribed's price is $149/mo, not $99 — the sent price wins"). SKILL.md's price reference also carries a $199 line for bigger profiles (2026-08-26); that number is NOT part of any approved answer. A prospect above SKILL.md's tier line still gets the $149 draft, labelled `needs Alfonso` with the note "above tier line, confirm $149" so he can change one number rather than write a reply.
- **What $149 covers:** their app under their own name, built on their material; the AI companion trained on their content; hosting; the app store submission; the notification and email loops that bring members back; and Tribed running all of that.
- **Ownership:** their content, their member list and their member data stay theirs. The method stays theirs; Tribed is the platform it runs on. The customised configuration and the AI setup built on their content (Angela Soltan asked exactly this; filled 2026-09-14 on Alfonso's go-ahead): everything derived from their material is theirs and leaves with them, meaning the content, the voice description, the questionnaire, the plans and the prompts written from their method. The engine it runs on, the app platform and the AI system underneath, is Tribed's and stays.
- **What they get on the way out (filled 2026-09-14, and deliberately narrower than "a full export").** Tribed assembles the handover for them: their member list with contact details and subscription status as a spreadsheet, plus their content, meaning plans, day plans, habits, quotes, products and the onboarding questionnaire. Say "we put it together for you", never "you export it yourself" and never "a full export" — the admin has spreadsheet exports for the member roster, the plans list and day plans, and everything past that is a manual pull. Member progress history (check-ins, habit tracking, reflections) is included when they ask for it: their member data is theirs, so it leaves with them (filled 2026-09-14 on Alfonso's go-ahead). It is a manual pull from the database, not a button, so say "we pull it for you" and never name a file format or a turnaround time.
- **The app-store listing (filled 2026-09-14).** The app is published under the coach's own Apple and Google accounts, so the listing is theirs and carries their name. Apps start life on Tribed's store accounts, so the coach does nothing technical while it is built and reviewed, then the app is transferred to their own developer account once it is approved and live. After the transfer the listing is theirs and stays exactly where it is, keeping its reviews and ratings; Tribed simply loses the ability to push updates to it. The coach needs their own Apple Developer membership (99 USD a year) and Google Play account (25 USD once) for the transfer to land. This is the documented flow in Tribed's own store-credentials guide, and three coach transfers have completed. **The one nuance, for a direct question only:** a coach who left before that transfer happened would be leaving an app still sitting on Tribed's account. Do not volunteer that; it answers a question nobody asked and prices a risk that the transfer removes.
- **The split:** Tribed runs hosting, app-store submission, the AI, and the notification and email loops. The coach owns the content, the voice, the method, and the relationship with their members. What Tribed needs from them in practice is their real material and their say on how it should feel.
- **The AI's role:** a companion between sessions, trained on the coach's material, answering in the coach's words. The coach stays the authority. It is not a standalone coach and it does not replace the session.
- **The method:** the app branches on the coach's rules, so what a member sees next depends on where that member is. It does not average the method into a linear task list. The member sees the next step; the rules behind it are the coach's. The smallest first experiment is one phase or one cohort, not the whole programme.

## The five answers

Each entry: what triggers it, the direction of the answer, the label it carries, and one English sample for register. Samples were re-checked 2026-09-14 against the core's structural-tells list: no feature-list runs, no "not X but Y", no announcing the point, no tailing negation, and the prospect's own name for their method wherever the sample allows it. Spanish replies use the same facts and labels in the prospect's Spanish per SKILL.md.

### 1. Price, and "what are the tiers?"

**Triggers:** "how much is it", "what does this run me", "pricing", "what are the plans / tiers", "send me the pricing tiers".

**Answer:** one number, $149 a month, setup free, month to month. One clause on what it covers. If they asked for tiers, do not say "there are no tiers" as a correction; state the one price and that it includes everything, and put anything custom into the walkthrough. Never quote $199. Never explain tier logic. Never defend the number with math; if they push back, the post-demo price direction in references/postdemo.md takes over.

**Label:** `ready`. (`needs Alfonso` only when the prospect sits above SKILL.md's tier line, note "above tier line, confirm $149".)

**Sample (post-demo, asked for tiers):**
> it's one price, $149 a month, and setup's on me. that's the app under your name and us running it, all in. if there's something specific you'd want beyond that, easier to go through it live. want me to show you around it? 15 minutes: [booking link]

### 2. Ownership, and what happens if they leave

**Triggers:** "who owns the data", "what happens to my clients' data", "IP", "what if I leave", "can I export", "what stays mine", "who owns the customised setup".

**Answer:** content, member list and member data stay theirs, and putting it in the app does not change that. The method is theirs; Tribed is the platform. The listing sits on their own store accounts and stays theirs with its reviews and ratings, and on the way out Tribed puts their member list and their content together for them. If they press on timing, the app is transferred to their developer account once it is approved and live, and until then it sits on Tribed's account so they do nothing technical. If they asked about the customised configuration or the AI setup: what was built from their material is theirs and leaves with them; the engine underneath is Tribed's and stays. Answer it straight; a deflection back to "just take a look" loses the buyer who asked. Then close on a small yes about their material.

**Label:** `ready`.

**Sample:**
> your content, your member list and your member data stay yours. the method's yours too, we're the platform it runs on. the app goes out on your own apple and google accounts, so the listing has your name on it, and if you ever walked you'd keep it, reviews and all, we'd just stop pushing updates. and we'd hand you your member list and your material on the way out. want me to put your real programme in it so you're judging the actual thing?

### 3. What Tribed runs vs what the coach does

**Triggers:** "what do you need from me", "what would I have to do", "what runs on your side and what's mine", "who manages the backend", "how much work is this for me". Angela Soltan's exact question, 2026-09-12.

**Answer:** name the split plainly in two sentences. Tribed: hosting, app store submission, the AI, the notification and email loops. Coach: content, voice, method, the member relationship. Then what Tribed needs from them in practice: their real material (the actual programme, not the public posts) and their say on how it should feel. Close on a yes about their material.

**Label:** `ready`.

**Sample:**
> the technical side is ours, hosting, the app store, the AI, the reminders going out. what's actually yours stays yours, the content, your voice, the method, your members. what I need from you is the real programme rather than what's public, and your say on how it should feel. want me to rebuild it on your actual material?

### 4. Is the AI a between-sessions companion or a standalone coach?

**Triggers:** "does the AI replace me", "what's the AI's role vs my coaching", "is it a coach on its own", "companion or standalone", "will my clients stop needing sessions".

**Answer:** companion, not a replacement. Trained on their material, so between sessions a member can ask it what to do with what the coach already taught them, in the coach's words. The coach stays the authority; it does not invent a method they do not have. Close on a "could use" yes about their people (references/voice.md rule: "could use", never a behaviour forecast).

**Label:** `ready`.

**Sample:**
> it sits between your sessions, it doesn't run them. it's trained on your material, so someone stuck on a wednesday can ask it what to do with what you already taught them and get it back in your words. you're still the one who decides what the method is. do you think that's something the people you work with could use between sessions?

### 5. "Will an app flatten my method into a generic task programme?"

**Triggers:** "generic task programme", "will it dumb my method down", "how is the diagnostic logic represented", "it's not a checklist, it depends on the person", "where does my judgement stay". The Marnus Marx objection (`raw/objection-app-will-flatten-my-method.md`), the best one this ICP has given.

**Answer:** take the worry seriously, it is the one that decides whether the build is worth doing for an expert. The app branches on their rules: what a member sees next depends on where that member is, the way it does when the coach is the one deciding. It does not average the method into a day-1-to-day-30 list. The member sees the next step; the rules behind it stay the coach's. Offer the smallest experiment that lets them refuse cheaply: one phase or one cohort, built on their actual rules. Name their method by its own name (the Reset, the Running Repairs), the way the sample does; "your method" in a reply to an expert reads as not having read them. If the ask came as a written scope, this is the DM cover and the proposal page carries the detail (see "How to use it", point 6).

**Label:** `ready`.

**Sample:**
> fair worry. it doesn't average the Reset into a day-1-to-day-30 checklist, it branches on your rules, so what someone sees next depends on where they are, same as when you're the one deciding. they see the next step, the reasoning stays yours. if you want to test that without betting the whole programme, we start with one phase or one cohort. want me to build that first phase on your rules?
